# Assessment

This course will use a version of specifications-based grading. In this course, there are 28 tokens to collect: 12 Statistics Tokens from the graded reviews (GRs), 9 Application Tokens, and 7 Professional Development tokens. The token breakdown is as follows

- 15 Statistics Tokens: GRs (with each token tied to a particular skill)
- 9 Application Tokens: 
	- 2 Tokens: Statistical Specialty
	- 3 Tokens: Semester-Long Group Project
	- 4 Tokens: Individual Homework Assignments
- 7 Professional Development Tokens:
	- 2 Tokens: Pre-Class Reading
	- 2 Tokens: Course Participation
	- 3 Tokens: Community Engagement




## Graded Reviews

There are four graded reviews (GRs) throughout the term plus one Final GR during the finals period. These are just moments in time to share what you current understanding of the course material is. There are 12 tokens, each representing core skills for the course. During each GR, you can attempt as many or as few tokens as you would like. Your goal, by the end of the semester, is to collect all 12 tokens. (Think of this like Pokemon, but for statistics)

The first GR will have the first 2 tokens available, the second will have the first 5, the third GR will have the first 9, and fourth GR will have all 12 tokens. The final will also have all 12 tokens. 

Put another way: 
- Tokens 1-2 will appear on GRs 1-4 plus the final
- Tokens 3-5 will appear on GRs 2-4 plus the final
- Tokens 6-9 will appear on GRs 3-4 plus the final 
- Tokens 10-12 will appear on GR 4 plus the final 

You do not need to (nor should you) attempt all tokens on each GR, but you do need to collect all 12 tokens by the end of the term. 


### Levels for Statistics Tokens on GRs

There are five levels for each statistics token: 
- P - Platinum: The student understands the material deeply enough to teach it to others. This represents a different level of profiency – the ability to explain, critique, and guide others. 
- G – Gold:  The student understands the material deeply enough to apply and interpret the concepts independently and correctly, with only minor or infrequent communication errors
- S – Silver: The student understands the material well enough to apply and interpret the concepts independently most of the time, with occasional minor justification errors. 
- B – Bronze: The student demonstrates partial understanding of the material; conceptual gaps, reasoning errors, or major misconceptions are present and must be addressed before the material can be applied reliably.
- CBA – cannot be assessed: The student has provided incoherent or incomplete evidence of understanding. 

Note these level descriptions match to the current version of DataSci 220 at USAFA. 


### Achieving Statistics Tokens on GRs

The 12 tokens associated to the GRs are tied to the 12 statistics tokens. The most recent level for each token is the recorded level. This means that if you earn a platnium level for token 1 on the first GR, then you never have to attempt this token again. But, if you earn silver for token 4 on the first challenge and then a bronze for token 4 on the second challenge, your current level for token 4 is bronze. 

:::{note}
You do not need to attempt all tokens on each GR. This means that you get to pick and choose which skills you are ready for on each GR. 
:::

For example, you may elect to just study token 1 for the first GR, then 2-4 for the second one, 5-8 for the third, and then 9-11 for the fourth one. For the final, you will have to attempt 12 plus any tokens you want to try again on the final. 

Alternatively, you may elect to study both tokens for the first GR to see how the system works, and then study 3-5 plus one from the first GR that didn't go as well. Then for the third one, you might again study three new ones (say 6-8) plus one or two that you want to "polish" from the first five tokens that you studied for the first two GRs. 


:::{warning}
You need to collect all 12 tokens by the end of the term. This means that you will need to start to think a bit more strategically as you prepare for each GR
:::


:::{note}
You **only** need to take the final GR if you _want_ to "upgrade" any token levels. 
:::

## Application Tokens

The 9 application tokens are attached to course activities where you apply your statistical knowledge. For the application tokens, there are four levels for each token: Gold, Silver, Bronze, CBA (can't be assessed)

### Statistical Specialty: 2 Tokens

In the first half of the semester, you will be asked to
select one statistical specialty: 1) communication and visualization, 2) mathematical foundations, 3)computational methods, or 4) statistical extensions. Each of these four specialties have two standards associated with them. You will complete a reading guide and assignment for each standard in your chosen specialty.

### Group Project: 3 Tokens

The tokens associated to the group project will be tied to rubrics. Like the SDS 100 project, there will be individual and group tasks. The grading rubrics will be introduced with the project. 

### Individual Homework Assignments: 4 Tokens

There are 8 homework assignments over the course. Each homework has two parts and are each worth 50 points. There are two homeworks per block that together represent one token. To earn a Gold for one homework token, you need to earn 80 or more points (of the 100 total). To earn a silver, you need to earn 60 or more, and to earn a Bronze, you need to earn 40 or more points. 

```{note}
Handing in incomplete homework is better than handing in nothing! 
```

Keep in mind that there are two parts to each assignment: Concept Checks and Core Competencies. The Concept Check questions can be done immediately after each class. 

## Professional Development Tokens

The 7 professional tokens are attached to course activities where you develop the habits associated with developing as a statistician and as a professional. 


### Pre-Class Reading: 2 Tokens

During the first three blocks of the course, you will be given a **printed** reading guide to complete for each lesson. The two tokens associated to the pre-class readings are based on your good-faith completion of these reading guides. 


### Engagement & Class Preparation: 2 Tokens

Two of these habit tokens will be earned with student input. In the sixth week of the course and during the last week of classes, you will be asked to complete a self-evaluation of your class engagement in context of the class engagement list. Your self-evaluations will be a critical part of determining the levels associated to these three tokens, but not the only determining factor. 

### Community Engagement: 3 Tokens

As a placed-based learning community, there are a number of co-curricular and extra-curricular events at Smith each week. As part of your work for this course, you are expected to engage in the out-of-class learning opportunities. 

The expectation is that you will attend 4 events this semester. Pre-approved eligible events include: 

* Any SDS or CSC Seminar
* The SDS Presentation of the Major
* The CSC Presentation of the Major
* Any Event that the instructor advertises on our slack (with the tag "community-engagement")
* At most one student-led event, such as a sporting event or a student-org event

If there is an event that you would like to attend towards this expectation that is not listed above, please reach out to me on slack. 

In early Decemeber, you will be asked to reflect on  your community engagement as it relates to you growth as a statistician and as a learner. Your tokens are assigned based off: attendance at your four events (2 tokens) and the quality of your reflection (1 token). 




## Flexibility Systems

This course has a number of flexbility systems built in:
- Notice that for homework and reading guides you only need to complete about 80% of the work in each division to earn a top score for that division. 
- For the statistics tokens on the GRs, you have between 2 and 5 attempts for each token. This means that you have time to iteratively study, attempt, reflect, and reattempt for each token. 

### Latework

All work is due at 10am on the date indicated on the Detailed Course Schedule. This is a great moment to note the due dates of all assignments in the system (ie. planner, calendar, etc) that you use to keep track of your work. (If you want to really stretch here, do this for all major assignments in all your courses.) 

In general, work should not be late. Later course content builds on previous content, and if one is spending time on an assignment that is passed due, then there is less time for the next assignment. Additionally the grading for this course has a number of flexibility systems in place, and I encourage that you take advantage of them as needed. 

This all being said, if you find yourself in need of a bit more time, send me a brief slack message at least a day **before** the work is due. In your message include 1) what flexibility systems you’ve already used (see above), 2) why it is important to your learning that you get an extension, 3) propose a new due date for yourself, and 4) why the proposed extension will not hinder your ability to get the next assignment done on time. Your message should be _no more than seven sentences long._ 

### Missing a GR

The self-schedule GR windows are listed on the Detailed Course Schedule. That being said, things do come up and you may be in a position where you need to miss a GR. Under this grading system, you are working to demonstrate you most complete understanding of the course material by the end of the term. So you can miss a GR for any reason knowing that you can attempt the tokens from the missed challenge on a later GR. 

:::{warning}
Missing two or more GRs is not advisable. 
:::

This being said, if something comes up, I would rather you reach out and check in, than not reach out. 


## Turning Tokens into Letter Grades

This course uses a contract system, in that your instructor will **guarantee** you grades based off the following process. This system is informed by previous iterations of this version of the course both here at Smith and at USAFA. 

Here's how it works: 

- For the contract grading, Platinums and Golds are treated as the same. This means that you are **never** required to earn Platinums. 
- Your final grade is determined with a base GPA score and then adjustments
- The following guarantees _may_ be lowered but they will _not_ be raised. 

The following table shows the guaranteed minimum final grade corresponding to the GPA points:

| Letter Grade    | Requirement |

| 4.0     | At least 8 Golds, remainder Silver and Bronze with a max of 1 Bronze  |  
| 3.0     | At least 6 Golds, remainder Silver and Bronze with a max of 3 Bronze |
| 2.0     | At least 6 Golds or Silvers, remainder Bronze with no CBA's |
| 1.0     | At least 4 Golds or Silvers, remainder Bronze with no more than 2 CBA's |

Higher levels can count for lower level totals, but not vice versa. For example, a silver level can count towards your bronze total but a silver level cannot count towards your gold total.
